# First-round equity question framework

Use this framework to create a short management-discussion guide, not a diligence RFI. Ask 6 to 10 questions and never exceed 10.

## Objective

Help an equity investor decide whether the market, area, opportunity and partnership are compelling enough to proceed to detailed VDR diligence. Test the investment story before testing the documents.

## Question sequence

Draft one clear question for each material theme. Combine or omit themes when appropriate.

1. **Market thesis:** Why is the market attractive now, what structural need supports storage, and why should the opportunity persist as competition grows?
2. **Regional thesis:** Why is this region or grid area better or different from alternatives, considering renewable buildout, constraints, market access and competing storage?
3. **Distinctive project advantage:** What gives the opportunity an edge in location, grid position, timing, scale, development maturity or strategic relevance?
4. **Duration and design logic:** Why is the proposed power, duration and technology the right configuration for the intended market need?
5. **Revenue strategy:** What is the preferred route to market over the asset life, why is it superior, and how can the project adapt if market conditions change?
6. **Equity return logic:** At a high level, what capital, return and cash-flow profile is expected, and which assumptions create most of the value?
7. **Delivery logic:** What are the few decisive milestones to reach operations, which is hardest, and why is the timetable credible?
8. **Partner proposition:** What role should the investor or strategic partner play, what value should it add, and how should economics, control and risk be shared?
9. **Scalability:** Is this a one-off asset or the foundation of a repeatable regional or national platform?
10. **Downside and conviction:** What could make the investment unattractive even if the project is delivered, and what protects the downside?

## Drafting standard

- Lead with market and regional logic, not project paperwork.
- Use plain, open questions that invite management to explain the thesis in its own words.
- Keep each question to one decision-relevant idea, with at most one closely related follow-up clause.
- Refer to project specifics only when they illuminate the investment thesis or a major execution assumption.
- Avoid internal dashboard terms, analyst conclusions and loaded wording.
- Do not assign priorities or request evidence.

## Deferred to the VDR round

Do not ask for permits, contracts, filings, models, invoices, warranties, legal opinions, data-room documents or other verification materials in the first-round list. Keep those needs in the gap report's close-out actions for later diligence.

## Removal test

Delete or combine a question when it:

- does not help decide whether to invest in the market, region, opportunity or partnership;
- asks primarily for evidence rather than the investment rationale;
- duplicates another theme; or
- takes the list above 10 questions.
