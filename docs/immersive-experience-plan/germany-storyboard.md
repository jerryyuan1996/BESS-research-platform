## 1. Scope, argument and reading route

The story explains the **current infrastructure-fund call and play**, read from the briefing model and published German view, not the 16 October content document. At the 31 October check: call ID `opportunistic`, rendered **Situational / 择机投入**; play `de-toll-4h`; entry at ready-to-build (REPO-DE-LIVE). The view's 30 October score note explicitly retains the call even though a relative ranking change crosses a score band. A score is not permission to replace the analyst's call. Other lenses have different calls; this script does not generalise the fund verdict to them.

**Argument:** Germany's deeply traded power market offers a merchant route; the current standalone four-hour design has no established adequacy-payment line. The model favours four hours over six on value, while the fund's toll reference trades a lower return for contractual revenue during its term. Acquisition still needs a signed contract and a priced network-cost exposure. Price swings explain the opportunity, not an assured margin.

The v1.1 spine survives, with evidence-led corrections. The liquidity superlative is supported for German power futures, not for the entire storage market. Capacity procurement is now legislated; the present long-duration test excludes this standalone design, while later participation remains conditional. “Extra hours are unpaid” becomes “their added earnings do not justify their cost in the current model”. Grid relief is never a revenue line in the story. Claim results and exact locators are in the source register.

Alternative explanation to retain: market depth and flexibility need can attract enough batteries, interconnection, flexible demand and other resources to reduce spreads. A firm toll can transfer some price risk but introduces counterparty, performance, cost-allocation and expiry risk. A final grid-fee order, a different best-duration result, a qualifying capacity award or different executed toll terms would reopen the script; none changes the model in this task.

Thirteen shots use the WA format and shared fixed-view isometric SVG kit. No scene is a load-flow calculation or project design. Ordinary scroll, chapter links, keyboard operation and reduced motion carry the same content; a reader never waits for animation.

### Rules for every shot

- **Figures:** every page-facing amount, percentage, duration, date and plotted numerical label is a `‹bound: …›` token. Section 9 identifies its existing result/source path. Tokens are an authoring contract, not code added here. No fallback number or model figure in prose. History dates bind source metadata; financial figures bind results, never thesis strings.
- **Teaching controls:** change era, schematic conditions, battery preset or route emphasis only. They do not change underwriting inputs, the selected market-page case or the published call. The longer battery is a teaching comparison, not a priced longer toll.
- **Evidence:** one marker and one “What would change it” line per shot, in both languages. Fair is the current ceiling for sourced material awaiting analyst review. Example is a teaching label, not a substitute evidence marker. Rights-blocked observations are withheld, not relabelled Thin.
- **History:** share the six-node timeline H-DE-01–06. A node expands event/source separately from “Why it matters”. Keep decision, effective date, completion and target separate. Draw future plans/scenarios dashed beyond the checked-today boundary; draw the enacted capacity law as an actual event.
- **Navigation:** retain shot/chapter/evidence position across Back, refresh and language change. Only one signature chart, reused by highlighting rather than spawning a second financial chart. The end links to the market page; it creates no second Bankable case.
- **Language:** all proposed page text, including titles, questions, controls, feedback, markers, legends and empty states, is paired below or in section 8. Scene/authoring instructions are English metadata, not additional untranslated page copy. Chinese is a draft for the analyst's G-1 review.
