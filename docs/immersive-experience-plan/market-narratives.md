## 1. A shared causal structure with different market stories

All markets use six chapters, but a template must not reduce every story to renewable growth followed by battery profits.

| Chapter ID | Investor's question | Required causal link | Reviewable output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `origins` | Why did this system develop here? | Geography, resources, settlement, industry and system/interconnection boundary | Labelled map; one geographic investment implication |
| `choices` | Why were these assets and rules chosen? | Constraint at the time -> decision -> inherited infrastructure/institution -> present consequence | 4-6 historical nodes, with evidence and separate causal interpretation |
| `tensions` | What is difficult today? | Distinguish total energy, timing, network location and rapid system response | One dominant tension, a secondary complication, and auditable 2D evidence |
| `battery` | What can a battery change? | Service -> power/energy/SOC/location/availability limits -> competing resource | Controlled with/without demonstration and station cutaway |
| `payment` | Who pays, on what conditions? | Service -> buyer/market -> qualification/award -> performance -> settlement | Payment path with project evidence status and incompatible obligations |
| `investment` | What do I own and when might I be paid? | Entry object -> capital/delivery/qualification -> cash flow/debt -> downside/exit | Current-case result bridge, missing diligence and value-weakening condition |

The draft theme should survive two challenges: “Could the same opening describe any other market?” and “What evidence would show this explanation is wrong or incomplete?” If either answer is weak, revise before commissioning assets.

## 2. Story-card standard and completion states

Each short card should fit approximately 250-400 words after research. It must contain the fields below, even if an unresolved field is explicitly marked pending.

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Identity and boundary | Allocation ID, market name, physical system/zone, shared research owner and applicable rule period |
| One-sentence distinction | A local causal argument in plain language; no return claim |
| Historical path | 4-6 candidate nodes; verified events separately identified from pending dates and inferred causes |
| Present tension | Main problem plus a secondary constraint; how energy/timing/location/response differ |
| Battery contribution and limit | A specific service, physical boundary and at least one alternative resource |
| Payment and investability | Buyer/market, access/award/contract/performance gates, model versus project evidence |
| Value weakening | Competition, network change, new flexibility, demand shift, rule change or contract expiry |
| Evidence, uncertainty and next action | Claim/source IDs, missing datasets/clauses, reviewer status and the next diligence question |

All 18 entries below are **proposed theme cards, editorial status `proposed`**. The canonical allocation IDs were verified in the repository; most story claims have not been refreshed in this task. A few narrow pilot foundations are separately recorded as verified in [the source register](source-register.md#verified-claim-seeds). A market's existing model readiness is not story-card completion.

Priority means production order: **P0** WA complete pilot; **P1** DE/FI distinct-story validation; **P2** Italy mechanism validation; **P3** reusable extension candidates. Within P3, the suggested cohorts can change after measured pilot effort. They are not investment rankings or commitments to concurrent production.

## 4. Detailed-script delivery standard for WA, Germany and Finland

| Deliverable | Required minimum | Publication gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market thesis | One distinctive causal sentence, one alternative explanation and one disconfirming condition | Research and investment interpretation reviewed |
| History | 4-6 nodes with evidence for events; cause/interpretation separately flagged | No invented date or future event shown as completed |
| Six-chapter script | Each chapter has question, proposed short copy, evidence, service/payment/investment bridge and a value limit | Each central claim sourced and reviewed |
| Shot sheets | Every shot has question, copy, objects, operation, feedback, 2D evidence, investment meaning, misreading boundary, mobile equivalent and state effects | No 3D-only essential content; no hidden model mutation |
| Evidence pack | Source/claim/calculation/source-to-shot registers with exact locators, versions, gaps and rights | Quantitative labels reproducible; unknowns withheld |
| Design pack | Desktop/mobile annotated views; normal, loading, error, reduced-motion and no-WebGL states | Bilingual overflow, focus and information hierarchy reviewed |
| Investment handoff | Case identity, supported controls, result bindings, contract status and first-revenue sequence | Reconciliation against existing financial outputs |
| Review record | Electricity pass, investment pass, translation pass and at least one novice-investor test iteration | Critical misreading corrected and retested |

## 5. Germany: spatial constraints without invented revenue

Proposed thesis: **The location of electricity and the ability to move it can matter as much as its total amount, but a useful battery location is not itself a payment contract.** `CL-DE-01` has a checked operator foundation; the wider historical sequence and financial bridge require research.

Candidate historical nodes to investigate: resource/industrial geography; legacy network and generation choices; renewable expansion and policy decisions; interregional corridor planning/delivery; operational redispatch development; present network/market reforms. Dates, geographic precision and causation remain pending.

| Chapter | Draft script beat and investor question | Scene / operation / feedback | 2D evidence and research still required | Investment meaning and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origins | “Where are resources and electricity users, and how did that geography emerge?” | Toggle labelled resource and load layers; retain one geographic frame | Dated generation/load geography and boundary rights; no unsourced exact plant pins | Location creates a research question; the map is not a project siting recommendation |
| Historical choices | “Why did supply and transmission develop on different schedules?” | Step through 4-6 dated layers; distinguish completed lines from plans | Primary policy/network chronology and evidence of lead times | Path dependence affects opportunity duration; a plan is not a completed solution |
| Today's tensions | “Why can traded electricity still encounter a transmission bottleneck?” | Show a constrained corridor; switch to redispatch explanation without simulated MW | SRC-DE-01/02; dated operational measures and definitions still to extract | System cost and asset revenue are separate; no cost-to-income allocation |
| Add a battery | “What can local storage change, and when is it insufficient?” | Compare storage, network expansion and flexible demand in qualitative presets | Connection/dispatch restrictions, battery limits and any local-service terms | Battery value depends on site, schedule and rights; no drag-to-raise-IRR mechanic |
| Who pays | “Which actual market or counterparty pays this asset?” | Open separate trading/reserve/contract paths; mark unverified routes | Governing participation, tariffs, prequalification, contract and stacking rules | Keep congestion relief and commercial eligibility distinct; no implied congestion rent |

Reuse the WA host, station cutaway, payer path and cash-flow presentation. Add only the spatial explanation component where necessary. Acceptance must include a participant saying why high redispatch costs do not establish the battery's revenue.

## 6. Finland: response requirements and competitive capture

Proposed thesis: **Enough annual energy does not remove the need for continuous balance; a battery's fast response earns revenue only through eligible products, competitive procurement and deliverable obligations.** `CL-FI-01` has a checked operator foundation. Product economics, historical causation and project capture remain separate work.

Candidate historical nodes to investigate: Nordic interconnection and market integration; generation and industrial-demand development; recent variable-output and flexible-resource changes; product/market integration reforms; actual reserve-price and eligible-supply changes; future electrification scenarios. Historical dates and current observations must be sourced separately from the Fingrid vision scenarios.

| Chapter | Draft script beat and investor question | Scene / operation / feedback | 2D evidence and research still required | Investment meaning and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Origins | “How does Finland connect to a wider electricity system?” | Regional context plus selectable generation/load/interconnection layers | Official geography, observation years and shared Nordic context | Avoid treating an interconnected system as the WA island example |
| Historical choices | “Which changes produced today's balance requirements?” | Timeline separates observed generation/demand changes from future scenarios | Dated primary history; SRC-FI-02 provides scenario context only | A future demand pathway does not guarantee reserve demand or project income |
| Today's tensions | “Why does an annual energy total not explain balance at each moment?” | Change timescale from year/day to conceptual response; retain ordinary units | SRC-FI-01; verified event data if any real frequency trace is used | Energy adequacy, rapid response and longer activation are different capabilities |
| Add a battery | “Which response can one battery sustain?” | Select a product/direction, then show power headroom and energy window | Product-specific technical requirements, qualification tests and asset limits | Fast hardware alone is insufficient; symmetric/asymmetric and duration terms must be verified |
| Who pays | “What is bought, and what can this asset actually capture?” | Separate capacity payment, activation energy and costs; increase competing eligible supply in a qualitative teaching preset | Governing terms; observed prices and eligible volumes; capture method and fees | Falling prices/award hours can weaken value; no assumption of every-hour award or unlimited stacking |
| The investment | “Does the captured margin support capital and financing?” | Current-case gross-to-equity bridge, downside, contract status and missing evidence | Existing result path; project terms, tariffs, degradation and finance assumptions | Market prices are observations; financing and contract coverage require project evidence |

PR #72 provides infrastructure for reserve-price observations; #73 and the subsequent data commit now provide the first stored snapshots. Treat these as observed price evidence, not as a pre-approved captured-revenue model. Check production freshness, observation/delivery timing and product definitions before using quantitative charts. Keep the annual energy view, frequency/response teaching view and EUR/MW/h price chart visually distinct.

Mobile alternative: a timescale selector, static response ladder, power/energy allocation cards and a compact gross-to-equity chart. A participant must be able to explain why both “the system needs reserves” and “reserve prices may fall” can be true.

## 7. Italy validation and wider expansion gates

After the DE/FI reuse exercise, give Italy a shared geography/history opening and an explicit commercial fork. Each fork needs its own buyer, qualification/award, term, construction/performance obligation, energy/dispatch rights, settlement deductions, expiry and project-evidence status. A visual switch must preserve which route the investment case uses and must not combine alternative contracts by default.

For subsequent markets, publish only after completing the same card and evidence contract. A shared renderer may accelerate production, but each market needs independent narrative, unit/time, payment, bilingual, mobile, numeric and understanding review. Record actual research/design/implementation/review time during the first four markets and use that evidence to set batch sizes.
